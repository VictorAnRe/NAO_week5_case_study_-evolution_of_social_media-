Social media, which can be defined as “forms of electronic communication (such as websites for social networking and microblogging) through which users create online communities to share information, ideas, personal messages, and other content (such as videos)” (Merriam-Webster, n.d.), has been completely transformed from the rudimentary telegraph to the modern-day social media that dictate the common form of interaction and digital commerce.  

In 1943, the first ever long-distance telegraph system was completed (60 km long), and the first public of the device was initiated (McGillem, 2024). Then, in 1969, the technology that most modern social media originate from, the Advance Research Projects Agency Network or the ARPANET, a digital network that allowed communication between 4 universities to share information. Based upon this technology, the most recent predecessor for the creation of social media was made with a nationwide digital network called NSFNET (Featherly, 2016), which is considered to be web 1.0, the first iteration of the internet that focused of static websites that one could read from but not interact with it. Finally, a decade later in 1997, SixDegrees.com became public on the internet, becoming the first ever social media platform to exist (Maryville University, 2020). Alongside the release of the popular social media website, Myspace, the era of web 2.0, interactive websites and social media, began.  

From this point onwards, the arrival of what most would consider “the modern internet” to be present and readily available to a large portion of the global population, especially with the most popular social media websites and eventually mobile applications, in the post PC era, such as YouTube, Twitter, Instagram and TikTok. 

Beyond reforming social communication and information dissemination, the evolution of social media represents a gigantic change in how business and commerce are done in enterprise computing infrastructure. The transformation of this infrastructure can be observed through the change from traditional relational databases to distributed NoSQL storage architectures, such as Facebook and Twitter, and data streaming pipelines (Dražena Gašpar and Mirela Mabić, 2017). This shift of revolutionary development of software infrastructure was not limited to data streaming, rather it expands to the integration of machine learning, causing computing logic to change from user queries to predictive AI engines from available data of user behaviour in social media. In an enterprise viewpoint, these faucets established multi-billion-euro digital economies, which focused on programmatic ad auctions, data commodification, and hyper-targeted consumer marketing (Kumar et al., 2025). Due to all of this, it has led to the foundation of modern cloud-scale infrastructure for enterprise platforms of the modern era.

## Ethics 1 - Privacy + exploiting data <!-- (141 words) --> 
Privacy has continued to be a huge ethical issue throughout all of social media’s history; however, the scale and use of personal data collection have changed drastically over recent years. In the early days, Myspace encourages its users to share their personal information about themselves like photos, interests, details about their personal lives (occupations, relationships etc.) and faced criticism with the way in which this information was shared with advertising agencies which resulted in a legal battle in 2012 (FTC, 2012).  Years later as Facebook grew in popularity, user data became much more valuable and used for much more malicious purposes when during the 2016 US election, Cambridge Analytica was paid to “improperly” collect personal data from millions of users for voter profiling (FTC, 2019) showing the contrast on how data exploitation was done in the beginning and in modern times. 

## Ethics 2 – Algorithms + engagement + wellbeing <!-- (178 words) --> 
As social media has evolved overtime, the way in which users connect with others and consume content on social media has changed. On older platforms, people connecting and finding content themselves, newer social medias like Instagram and TikTok rely more on algorithms and recommendations to create personalized feeds for their users (TikTok, 2020). This is put in as it makes it easier for the user to find content they would enjoy and helps provide opportunities to smaller creators. However, unfortunately there are some dangerous downsides to consider, as features such as endless scrolling and autoplay encourage excessive usage and time on these platforms. This causes concerns around social media’s impact on users’ mental wellbeing, especially for younger users (Jia & Wang, 2026). Furthermore, it has been proven that there is a clear association between excessive social media use and mental health difficulties including symptoms such as anxiety and depression (Office of the Surgeon General, 2023). Overall, social media companies should consider whether maximizing user attention for better advertising opportunities justifies the potential risks to their user’s mental health. 

## Possible improvements <!-- (179 words) --> 
Now, to help address these ethical concerns, social media companies should start by better explaining to users how their data is collected and used, which would help reduce uninformed/unethical data collection. There should also be features such as screen time settings and the ability to choose non-personalised feeds which could help reduce mental harm from excessive use and exposure to addictive content (European Commission, 2025). Also, much stronger restrictions on minors signing up for platforms is required however, how this is to be implemented is under constant debate at the moment as governments are using it as an excuse to infringe on the privacy of adult users as well through requiring face scans and government IDs (Ofcom 2026).

# Societal Viewpoint Analysis

Social media has significantly influenced modern society and changed the way people communicate, access information and participate in communities. Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube have become an important part of everyday life for billions of people. From a societal perspective, social media has created both positive and negative impacts that affect individuals, communities and society as a whole.

## Communication and Global Connectivity

One of the most significant societal impacts of social media is improved communication. Before social media platforms became popular, people relied mainly on phone calls, text messages and emails to stay connected. Today, social media allows users to communicate instantly through messages, comments, posts and video calls. Family members and friends can maintain relationships regardless of geographical location.

Social media has also enabled global connectivity. People can interact with individuals from different countries and cultures, allowing the exchange of ideas and experiences. Online communities have developed around shared interests, hobbies and professional goals, creating opportunities for collaboration and social support.

## Education and Access to Information

Social media has become an important source of information and learning. Educational content is widely available through platforms such as YouTube, Instagram and TikTok. Students can watch tutorials, access study resources and learn new skills through online creators and educational communities.

Another important benefit is the rapid sharing of information. News organisations, educational institutions and government agencies use social media to communicate updates and important information to the public. This provides users with access to information more quickly than many traditional methods of communication.

In addition, social media encourages self-directed learning. Users can explore topics that interest them and gain knowledge outside formal educational settings. This has made learning more accessible to people of different ages and backgrounds.

## Social Awareness and Community Engagement

Social media has increased awareness of social, environmental and political issues. Campaigns and public awareness initiatives can reach millions of users within a short period of time. Individuals and organisations use social media platforms to promote causes, encourage discussion and share information with large audiences.

Social media has also increased community engagement by allowing users to participate in discussions, express opinions and contribute to public conversations. Fundraising campaigns, community projects and awareness movements frequently use social media to attract support and engage with the public.

As a result, social media has provided people with new ways to participate in society and engage with issues that affect their communities.

## Negative Effects on Society

Despite its benefits, social media also presents several challenges. One major issue is cyberbullying. Social media platforms can be used to harass, threaten or embarrass individuals online. Cyberbullying can negatively affect mental health and emotional wellbeing.

Another concern is misinformation. False or misleading information can spread quickly through social media platforms. Because information can be shared rapidly, users may have difficulty determining whether content comes from reliable sources. This can create confusion and influence public opinion.

Mental health concerns have also been linked to excessive social media use. Constant exposure to idealised images and lifestyles may contribute to anxiety, stress and low self-esteem. Excessive screen time may also reduce face-to-face interaction and negatively affect personal relationships.

## Overall Impact on Society

Overall, social media has transformed how people communicate, learn and participate in society. It has improved global connectivity, increased access to information and created opportunities for education and community engagement. However, challenges such as cyberbullying, misinformation and mental health concerns demonstrate that social media can also have negative consequences. From a societal perspective, social media remains one of the most influential technological developments of the modern era, offering both opportunities and challenges for society.
